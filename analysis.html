4. Checklist coverage across the register

  • 01Karat stated. 30 of 30 rows publish a karat. None of those values is a measured purity.
  • 02Explicit solid gold. 13 of 30 rows use the words solid gold on their own product page. The other rows are still listed, because a karat alone is not evidence of solid construction (F-001).
  • 03Hallmark and maker’s mark. 1 of 30 rows document a physical karat stamp inside the band, and 0 document a maker’s/manufacturer’s mark (F-002).
  • 04Metal weight. 7 of 30 rows publish a stone-free metal weight; 3 more publish only a gross item weight that includes stones (F-003, F-028).
  • 05Price currency. 10 of 30 rows carry an assigned amount whose currency is supported by the retailer’s own statement. The rest show the page text verbatim and stay unassigned (F-025).
  • 06Independent testing permission. 0 of 30 rows have written permission captured for third-party testing or return after testing (F-021).
  • 07Physical verification. 0 of 30 rings were weighed, XRF-tested, or inspected in person (F-024).

5. What this analysis must not be used for

The melt figures are conditional arithmetic, not appraisals, offers, or resale floors. A ring can legitimately cost several times its metal value because of design, labour, setting, brand and margin — and a suspiciously low price is the direction that deserves the extra questions (checklist item: unusually cheap pricing is a red flag). Nothing here establishes that a ring is solid gold, that its stated karat is accurate, or that a seller will honour a return.
