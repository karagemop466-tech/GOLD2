1. Karat → pure-gold content (computed)

Formula: pure-gold fraction = karat ÷ 24. Pure (fine) gold is 24 karat.

2. Troy ounce (exact conversion)

1 troy ounce = 31.1034768 grams (exactly). Read on the official LBMA “Troy Ounce” page (fetched 2026-10-22). The same figure is published by NIST for precious-metal conversions.

Sources: LBMA — The Troy Ounce · NIST — Conversion Factors for Precious Metal Sales.

3. How the gold benchmark price works (method only — no snapshot)

The LBMA Gold Price is set twice daily in auctions (10:30 and 15:00 London time), independently operated and administered by ICE Benchmark Administration (IBA), in US dollars. Sterling/euro figures are indicative, for settlement only. Benchmarks are not published on weekends or UK bank holidays. Real-time/historical benchmark data requires a licence from IBA. Read on the official LBMA page (fetched 2026-10-22).

Sources: LBMA Precious Metal Prices · About LBMA Daily Auction Prices. Enter your own quote in the calculator with its date/time.

4. Hallmark & maker’s-mark law (summaries — official text controls)

  • Fineness tolerances — 15 U.S.C. § 295. Gold articles must not test more than 3 parts per thousand below the stamped fineness (solder-free portion); the whole article including solder must not test more than 3 ppt below (watchcases, flatware) or 7 ppt below (all other articles) the mark. Read on govinfo — 15 U.S.C. Chapter 8 (fetched 2026-10-22).
  • Maker’s mark required — 15 U.S.C. § 297(b). Anyone who applies (or imports an article bearing) a quality mark stating gold content must also apply their registered trademark or name — by the same means, in type at least as large, as close as possible to the mark. Read on govinfo — 15 U.S.C. Chapter 8, § 297 (fetched 2026-10-22).
  • Gold content — FTC 16 CFR § 23.3. Misrepresenting gold presence, quantity, or karat fineness is unfair/deceptive. A karat designation must immediately precede “Gold” with at least equal conspicuousness (e.g., “14 Karat Gold”). Hollow-center gold-alloy products must disclose it and must not be called “solid”. Gold plate/filled/overlay/rolled and electroplate have specific fineness, thickness, and disclosure rules. Read on eCFR — 16 CFR Part 23 (fetched 2026-10-22).
  • Vermeil — FTC 16 CFR § 23.4. Sterling-silver base coated on all significant surfaces with gold (or ≥10K gold alloy) of reasonable durability and at least 2.5 microns of fine gold; a base-metal layer under the gold must be disclosed. Read on eCFR — 16 CFR Part 23 (fetched 2026-10-22).
  • Quality marks — FTC 16 CFR § 23.10. not line-by-line reviewed Linked for manual review: eCFR — 16 CFR Part 23.
