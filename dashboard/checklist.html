Gold ring — online buying checklist

Each requirement below shows how this site checks it and the official source behind the rule. “Project brief” = the research brief for this project (not an official external source).

  1. 1. Gold purity — confirm the karat (10K, 14K, 18K…)

    Higher karat = more gold. Recorded per ring exactly as the listing states it (see ring table).

    Rule sources: FTC Jewelry Guides, 16 CFR Part 23 (eCFR) · karat math · Project brief.

  2. 2. Solid gold — listing must explicitly say “solid gold”

    Not plated, vermeil, gold-filled, gold overlay, or gold-tone. Our finding: the five captured listings state karat metal but the fetched text does not contain “solid gold” — flagged F-SOLID-WORDING. Get written confirmation from the seller.

    Rule sources: 16 CFR § 23.3 (plate/filled/overlay/electroplate definitions; hollow-center items must not be called “solid”) · 16 CFR § 23.4 (vermeil) · Project brief.

  3. 3. Hallmark — karat stamp plus maker’s/manufacturer’s mark

    U.S. law requires anyone applying (or importing an article bearing) a gold quality mark to also apply their registered trademark or name — same means, at least as large, as close as possible to the mark. No captured listing describes the physical stamp — all flagged F-HALLMARK. Inspect after delivery, before wearing/resizing.

    Rule sources: 15 U.S.C. § 297(b) (govinfo) · FTC 16 CFR Part 23 · Project brief.

  4. 4. Weight — ring’s weight in grams

    A legitimate seller should be able to provide it. None of the five captured pages publish it — all blank, flagged F-WEIGHT. Ask the seller; have an independent jeweler weigh the ring on arrival. Distinguish total ring weight (metal + stones) from metal-only weight: melt math needs metal-only — never derive one from the other. Ask which figure any quote refers to.

    Rule source: Project brief. Conversion reference: LBMA troy ounce = 31.1034768 g exact.

  5. 5. Seller reputation — independent reviews, real contact info

    Verified contact details per retailer on the retailers page (phone, email, contact page — official links). Independent reviews/complaints research is not included in this pass — do it yourself before buying (see flagged).

    Rule source: Project brief. Contacts verified at official contact pages (linked per retailer).

  6. 6. Price — compare against gold weight + current gold value

    Use the calculator: melt value = metal-only weight × (karat ÷ 24) × (your spot quote ÷ 31.1034768). An unusually cheap price is a red flag. Our prices are 2026-10-22 captures, re-check live (F-PRICE-VOLATILITY).

    Rule source: Project brief. Benchmark method: LBMA Precious Metal Prices.

  7. 7. Return policy — returnable after inspection; watch resized/custom exclusions

    Per-retailer summaries with exclusions on the retailers page, each linked to the official policy. Blue Nile excludes resized-after-purchase/custom/engraved items; Costco inspects >1 ct diamonds via a gemologist.

    Rule sources: Blue Nile returns · Costco jewelry returns · Project brief.

  8. 8. Payment — prefer a credit card

    Purchase protection; avoid irreversible methods. Blue Nile advertises credit-card financing on product pages — full options live at checkout (confirm there).

    Rule source: Project brief. Observed: Blue Nile product pages (linked per ring).

  9. 9. Documentation — keep listing, receipt, description, purity claims

    Save the listing page (PDF/print), receipt/invoice, and any written seller claims about purity/weight. This site’s rows give you the exact source URL + capture date to file. Purchase-records checklist (project brief): the listing capture, the invoice/receipt, the seller’s written replies (weight? solid? returnable as configured?), photos of the hallmark/maker’s mark, any certificates (e.g., diamond grading report), and the independent test report (items 10–11).

    Rule source: Project brief.

  10. 10. Upon arrival — independent jeweler verification before wearing/resizing

    Take the ring to an independent jeweler first: verify purity and weight. Resizing before verification can void return eligibility (see item 7).

    Rule source: Project brief.

  11. 11. Best verification — XRF or another professional purity test

    Do not rely solely on the stamp. Note the legal tolerances: up to 3 ppt below mark (solder-free test) and up to 7 ppt for the whole article incl. solder (non-flatware).

    Rule sources: Project brief (XRF recommendation) · 15 U.S.C. § 295 tolerances (govinfo).

  12. 12. If the seller won’t let you independently verify/return it — don’t buy it

    Non-negotiable. Confirm the return path in writing for the exact item (including size/engraving choices) before paying.

    Rule source: Project brief.

The key, restated: a 14K/18K stamp by itself does not prove the ring is that purity. Safest approach = reputable seller + proper markings + reasonable price + independent testing after delivery. (Project brief.)
